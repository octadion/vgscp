\section{Claims withdrawn}
\label{sec:withdrawn}

Four statements from the submitted paper are withdrawn rather than softened, because measurement
contradicts them. Three were found only because the reviewers asked for more backbones and proper
intervals.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.4em]
\item \textbf{``Mondrian is $2$--$4\times$ more split-stable than marginal calibration.''} True on
the two backbones of the submission; false on four. Marginal's split-to-split SD is
$0.009$--$0.042$ against Mondrian's $0.023$--$0.034$, and in two of the eight cells Mondrian is the
\emph{less} stable of the two. Mondrian's advantage is in the level it attains, not in its variance.
\item \textbf{``The absolute efficiency range is narrow.''} The spread runs $0.036$ to $0.364$, and
a two-one-sided-test equivalence check rejects equivalence at a $0.10$ margin in six of eight cells.
\item \textbf{``Training buys efficiency, not coverage''} is now stated as conditional on Mondrian.
Under marginal calibration, training still moves coverage.
\item \textbf{The per-cell accuracy--set-size correlations.} Reported bare in the submission,
including a $-0.97$ and a $-1.00$. With cluster-bootstrap intervals they are uninformative in five
of eight cells, each resting on only three to five methods. The revision reports a direction, not
an estimate.
\end{enumerate}

The cause of the two retractions is worth stating precisely, because it is the kind of error that
survives review: the submitted analysis pooled each arm's (accuracy, divergence) points across
seeds \emph{and} calibration splits. ERM's solver is deterministic, so its per-seed accuracy is
identical to five decimals, while its across-split range is $0.02$--$0.03$. The accuracy axis ERM
appeared to span was therefore \emph{which evaluation rows were drawn}, not \emph{which model was
trained}, and matching on it compared ERM on unlucky draws against a robust arm on lucky ones.

\section{Corrections we made without being asked}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item A definitional inconsistency in the coverage gap (above).
\item A rendering defect in the predicted-group table, which declared eight columns and supplied
seven. All nine tables now pass a column-count check.
\item An empty-set disclosure: on DINOv2/Waterbirds the mean worst-group set size is below one
($0.926$--$0.964$), so a small fraction of prediction sets are empty. This is legitimate for split
conformal with a highly accurate model and coverage remains valid, but we report it rather than
clip sets to a minimum of one, which would hide it.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What did not change}

The central finding is unchanged and now rests on twice the cells and on a study in which the
representation itself is retrained. Under marginal calibration, worst-group coverage tracks the
training method and collapses as low as $0.509$; under Mondrian calibration it lands in
$0.854$--$0.886$ in every cell, and the cross-training spread never exceeds $0.024$. The
dissociation holds across all four backbones, across three conformity scores, across the
correlation-strength sweep, under predicted rather than true groups, and when the representation is
fine-tuned end-to-end.

What the revision changes is the strength of the surrounding language, the honesty of the
uncertainty, and the number of places a reader can check it.
