\section{Claims withdrawn}
\label{sec:withdrawn}

Four statements from the submitted paper are withdrawn rather than softened, because measurement
contradicts them. Three were found only because the reviewers asked for more backbones and proper
intervals.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.4em]
\item \textbf{``Mondrian is $2$--$4\times$ more split-stable than marginal calibration.''} True on
the two backbones of the submission; false on four. Marginal's split-to-split SD is
$0.007$--$0.043$ against Mondrian's $0.011$--$0.036$, and in five of the eight settings
Mondrian is the \emph{less} stable of the two. Mondrian's advantage is in the level it attains, not
in its variance.
\item \textbf{``The absolute efficiency range is narrow.''} The spread runs $0.036$ to $0.364$, and
an equivalence check leaves the spread above a $0.10$ margin in six of eight settings.
\item \textbf{``Training buys efficiency, not coverage''} is now stated as conditional on Mondrian.
Under marginal calibration, training still moves coverage.
\item \textbf{The per-setting accuracy--set-size correlations.} Reported bare in the submission,
including a $-0.97$ and a $-1.00$. With bootstrap intervals over the methods they are
uninformative in six of eight settings, each resting on only three to five methods. The revision reports a direction, not
an estimate.
\end{enumerate}

The cause of the two retractions is worth stating precisely, because it is the kind of error that
survives review: the submitted analysis pooled each method's (accuracy, divergence) points across
seeds \emph{and} calibration splits. ERM's solver is deterministic, so its per-seed accuracy is
identical to five decimals, while its across-split range is $0.008$--$0.026$ on Waterbirds and
$0.036$--$0.051$ on the CelebA settings it is retained in. The accuracy axis ERM
appeared to span was therefore \emph{which evaluation rows were drawn}, not \emph{which model was
trained}, and matching on it compared ERM on unlucky draws against a robust method on lucky ones.

\section{Further corrections}

One substantive change no review asked for: the coverage gap was defined inconsistently in
Section~3 of the submitted paper, contradicting both the implementation and that paper's own
appendix. It now reads $(1-\alpha)-\min_g\mathrm{cov}_g$, matching both (Section~\ref{sec:numbers}).

\section{What did not change}

The central finding is unchanged and now rests on twice the settings and on a study in which the
representation itself is retrained. Under marginal calibration, worst-group coverage tracks the
training method and collapses as low as $0.509$; under Mondrian calibration it lands in
$0.84$--$0.89$ in every setting, and the cross-training spread never exceeds $0.024$, all at APS. The
dissociation holds across all four backbones, across three conformity scores, across the
correlation-strength sweep, under predicted rather than true groups, and when the representation is
fine-tuned end-to-end.

What the revision changes is the strength of the surrounding language, how the uncertainty is
reported, and the number of places a reader can check it.
